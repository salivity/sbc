MIPI-CSI PCB Layout Guidelines for Custom SBCs

Routing high-speed Mobile Industry Processor Interface Camera Serial Interface (MIPI-CSI) lines on a custom single-board computer (SBC) requires strict adherence to high-speed PCB design principles. Because MIPI-CSI protocols—such as D-PHY and C-PHY—operate at gigabit speeds, minor routing flaws can lead to signal degradation, electromagnetic interference (EMI), and camera synchronization failures. This guide outlines the essential routing rules, covering impedance matching, length tuning, layer transitions, and shielding to ensure robust signal integrity.

1. Controlled Impedance Matching

MIPI D-PHY signals operate in both low-power (LP) single-ended mode and high-speed (HS) differential mode.

  • Maintain a target differential impedance of 100 Ω ± 10% for differential pairs (data and clock lanes).
  • Maintain a single-ended impedance of 50 Ω ± 10% for each trace relative to its reference ground plane.
  • Keep trace widths and inter-pair spacing consistent across the entire length of the run to prevent impedance discontinuities.

2. Intra-Pair and Inter-Pair Skew Matching

Timing alignment between the differential signals and between data/clock lanes is vital to prevent bit errors.

  • Intra-Pair Skew (P to N): Match the lengths within each differential pair as tightly as possible, ideally within ±0.15 mm (approx. 1 ps to 2 ps). Apply serpentine tuning at the point where the mismatch occurs, preferably near the driver or receiver.
  • Inter-Pair Skew (Data to Clock): Match the length of all data lanes relative to the clock lane within ±0.5 mm to ±1.0 mm, depending on the specific MIPI-CSI data rate and SoC specifications.

3. Continuous Reference Planes

High-speed signals require an uninterrupted return path to minimize loop inductance and EMI.

  • Always route MIPI-CSI traces over a solid, unbroken ground plane (GND).
  • Never route differential pairs across plane splits, power planes, or voids. Crossing a split creates large return loops, generating noise and signal distortion.
  • If a signal must change reference from one ground layer to another, place a ground stitching via immediately adjacent to the signal via pair.

4. Minimizing Vias and Layer Transitions

Every via adds parasitic capacitance and inductance that can disrupt high-speed transmission.

  • Route all MIPI lines on a single signal layer if possible (preferably on the top layer directly referenced to Layer 2 ground).
  • If layer transitions are unavoidable, ensure both the positive (P) and negative (N) traces change layers simultaneously using an identical via layout.
  • Remove unused via pads (non-functional pads) and consider back-drilling on thick boards to minimize via stubs.

5. Crosstalk Mitigation and Trace Separation

To protect weak analog-level differential signals from external noise:

  • Maintain a spacing of at least 3W to 5W (where W is the trace width) between MIPI pairs and other adjacent traces.
  • Keep MIPI differential lines well away from high-noise circuits, including switching power supplies (DC-DC buck/boost converters), oscillators, RF antennas, and high-current traces.
  • Route clock and data lanes parallel to each other with sufficient clearance between adjacent differential pairs to avoid inter-lane crosstalk.

6. Component Placement and Connector Interfaces

Discontinuities at connectors and passive components can degrade performance.

  • Place any required ESD protection diodes or series resistors as close to the connector as possible.
  • Select ESD diodes designed specifically for high-speed differential lines with ultra-low capacitance (< 0.3 pF).
  • Ensure the pad geometry of passives matches the trace width closely, or apply copper cutouts under component pads on the reference layer if needed to prevent local capacitive impedance dips.
