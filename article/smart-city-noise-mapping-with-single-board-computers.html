Smart City Noise Mapping with Single Board Computers

Single board computers (SBCs) have revolutionized smart city noise mapping by providing low-cost, scalable, and intelligent edge nodes for distributed acoustic monitoring. By processing audio signals locally, these compact computing devices enable municipalities to continuously capture high-resolution sound level data, categorize noise pollution sources, and generate dynamic urban acoustic maps without incurring excessive hardware costs or compromising citizen privacy.

Edge-Level Acoustic Processing and Data Reduction

Traditional sound monitoring systems rely on costly, specialized sound level meters that often log data locally or require significant bandwidth to transmit raw audio to centralized servers. SBCs—such as the Raspberry Pi, BeagleBone, or NVIDIA Jetson Nano—act as edge processors. Equipped with digital micro-electro-mechanical systems (MEMS) microphones or calibrated audio interfaces, they run real-time digital signal processing (DSP) algorithms to compute standardized acoustic metrics directly on the device. Metrics such as equivalent continuous sound levels (\(L_{eq}\)), day-evening-night levels (\(L_{den}\)), and \(1/3\)-octave band spectra are extracted locally, dramatically reducing the volume of data that needs to be transmitted over municipal networks.

Privacy Preservation by Design

Streaming raw continuous audio from public spaces raises significant regulatory and privacy concerns under frameworks like GDPR. Single board computers mitigate these risks through edge processing. Because the acoustic features and decibel measurements are calculated in real time, the raw audio data can be discarded instantly from the device's volatile memory. Furthermore, onboard machine learning models can classify sound events—differentiating between road traffic, rail transport, construction, aircraft, and human speech—without storing or transmitting any intelligible audio recordings.

High-Density Deployment and Cost Efficiency

Accurate urban noise mapping requires dense spatial granularity because sound propagation varies significantly due to buildings, street geometry, and vegetation. Single board computers provide a cost-effective alternative to proprietary Class 1 or Class 2 acoustic stations, which often cost thousands of dollars per unit. The affordability and compact form factor of SBCs allow cities to deploy vast sensor networks across streetlights, utility poles, and traffic signals. This high-density coverage enables continuous, 24/7 spatial mapping rather than relying on sparse measurements and theoretical sound propagation simulations.

Flexible Network Integration and Urban Management

Modern SBCs feature native support for diverse communication protocols, including Wi-Fi, Ethernet, cellular (4G/5G), and low-power wide-area networks like LoRaWAN. They can reliably transmit lightweight metric packets to centralized Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and smart city platforms. Urban planners and environmental agencies can use this real-time stream of acoustic data to identify noise pollution hotspots, optimize traffic management strategies, enforce environmental noise regulations, and evaluate the impact of infrastructure changes over time.
