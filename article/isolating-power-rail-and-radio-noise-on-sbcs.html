Isolating Power Rail and Radio Noise on SBCs

Single board computers (SBCs) integrate high-speed digital processors, switching power supplies, and sensitive radio frequency (RF) transceivers onto a compact printed circuit board. High-frequency switching noise and voltage ripple from power rails can easily couple into the RF circuitry, causing receiver desensitization, phase noise, and transmitter spectral mask violations. Mitigating this electrical interference requires a combination of low-noise regulation, passive filtering networks, strategic PCB layout, and physical shielding.

Dedicated Low-Dropout Regulators (LDOs)

Directly powering RF modules from switching DC-DC converters (buck/boost) introduces substantial switching spikes and ripple. The primary method to isolate the radio is placing an ultra-low-noise, high Power Supply Rejection Ratio (PSRR) LDO exclusively for the RF circuitry. The LDO acts as an active low-pass barrier, suppressing high-frequency ripple from the primary power rail before it reaches the RF transceiver.

Multi-Stage LC and Pi Filtering

Passive filtering placed directly on the power entry pins of the RF section attenuates both differential and common-mode conducted noise:

  • Ferrite Beads: Inserting high-impedance ferrite beads rated for the specific noise frequency band (typically 100 MHz to several GHz) effectively blocks high-frequency RF currents from traveling along the power line.
  • Pi Filters (C-L-C): Implementing a Pi network using a ferrite bead or inductor sandwiched between two parallel ceramic capacitors creates steep roll-off characteristics to isolate broadband switching harmonics.

Decoupling Capacitor Arrays

A single capacitor cannot filter broadband noise due to its self-resonant frequency (SRF). Using an array of ceramic decoupling capacitors placed in parallel provides low impedance across a wide frequency spectrum:

  • Bulk Capacitors (10 µF to 47 µF): Absorb low-frequency voltage transients.
  • Mid-Range Capacitors (0.1 µF to 1 µF): Filter standard digital switching transients.
  • Small RF Capacitors (10 pF to 100 pF): Provide low impedance at the operating frequency of the radio. These capacitors must be positioned as close as physically possible to the power pins of the RF IC, referencing a solid ground plane with minimal trace inductance.

PCB Layer Stackup and Ground Plane Partitioning

Improper return paths allow power rail noise to modulate the RF ground reference. Effective isolation techniques include:

  • Solid Ground Planes: Providing an uninterrupted reference plane beneath both the power distribution and RF sections minimizes loop area and ground bounce.
  • Moats and Keep-Out Zones: Creating isolation cuts in the power plane ensures noisy digital and switching power distribution paths do not cross underneath the RF transceiver or antenna feedlines.
  • Star Grounding: Routing separate power and ground paths back to a single source point prevents common-impedance coupling between digital power stages and the RF chain.

Strategic Physical Separation and Routing

Physical placement dictates the amount of capacitive and inductive cross-coupling:

  • Spatial Distance: Locating the main DC-DC inductors and switching nodes as far as possible from the RF front-end modules and antenna.
  • Shielded Traces: Using coplanar waveguides with ground vias along the RF paths and placing power traces on internal layers between solid ground planes (stripline configuration).

Metallic Shielding Cans (Faraday Cages)

Near-field magnetic and electric fields generated by switching power inductors can radiate directly into RF components over the air. Surface-mount metal shielding cans soldered over the RF module, or over the switching power supply itself, block radiated emissions and prevent direct electromagnetic coupling between the power converter and the radio.
