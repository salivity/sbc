Single Board Computers in Smart Parcel Lockers

Modern automated parcel delivery lockers rely on single board computers (SBCs) as their primary control units to manage secure storage, process user authentication, and maintain real-time cloud connectivity. These compact computing platforms bridge physical hardware components with logistics software networks, enabling automated, contactless last-mile deliveries. This article examines the vital functions SBCs perform in parcel locker operations, including hardware control, edge computing, network communication, and system reliability.

Centralized Hardware Control and Sensor Integration

The primary role of an SBC in a smart locker is to serve as the master hardware controller. Through General Purpose Input/Output (GPIO) pins, serial interfaces, and USB ports, the SBC coordinates multiple peripheral devices:

  • Electronic Lock Mechanisms: Controls multi-channel relay boards to unlock specific compartments upon command.
  • Sensor Arrays: Reads data from optical sensors, infrared detectors, and weight sensors to verify compartment occupancy and detect whether a door is open or closed.
  • User Interfaces: Drives touchscreens, displays user prompts, interfaces with physical keypads, and processes inputs from barcode/QR code scanners.
  • Surveillance and Security: Connects to onboard cameras to capture images or video logs during parcel drop-offs and customer pickups.

Edge Computing and Local Authentication

SBCs possess sufficient processing power to handle tasks locally at the edge rather than depending entirely on remote cloud servers. This capability ensures that automated lockers operate quickly and securely:

  • Offline Resilience: If network connectivity fails, the SBC can authenticate authorized delivery personnel and recipients using locally cached, encrypted access codes or public-key cryptography.
  • Media Processing: The board processes camera feeds, decodes QR codes in real time, and compresses diagnostic logs before transmitting them to save bandwidth.

Network Gateway and Cloud Synchronization

Automated parcel lockers must constantly communicate with central logistics management platforms. An SBC acts as the secure IoT gateway by managing network interfaces such as Ethernet, Wi-Fi, and 4G/5G cellular modems. It performs critical synchronization tasks:

  • Transmitting real-time locker status and availability to the central database.
  • Receiving dynamic access tokens and one-time passwords (OTPs) generated by courier systems.
  • Sending automated alerts to delivery dispatchers regarding mechanical faults, forced entries, or maintenance needs.

Cost Efficiency and Industrial Reliability

Compared to traditional industrial PCs, single board computers offer a low-power, compact, and cost-effective footprint. Their solid-state design eliminates moving parts, reducing failure rates in outdoor or variable-temperature environments. Furthermore, low energy consumption allows parcel lockers to operate efficiently 24/7 or run seamlessly on battery backup systems during power outages.
