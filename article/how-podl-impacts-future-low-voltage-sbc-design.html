How PoDL Impacts Future Low-Voltage SBC Design

Power-over-Data-Line (PoDL) technology is reshaping the architecture of low-voltage single-board computers (SBCs) by delivering DC power and bidirectional data over a single twisted pair of wires. This article examines how the integration of PoDL through Single Pair Ethernet (SPE) standards simplifies PCB layouts, reduces physical footprint and bill-of-materials costs, enhances thermal and power efficiency, and unlocks new deployment possibilities for edge computing, industrial automation, and embedded IoT devices.

Miniaturization and PCB Real Estate Optimization

Traditional SBCs rely on separate paths for power and connectivity—such as dedicated USB-C ports, DC barrel jacks, or bulky multi-pair RJ45 connectors required for standard Power-over-Ethernet (PoE). PoDL eliminates these redundant interfaces by using a single pair of conductors (IEEE 802.3bu and IEEE 802.3cg).

Replacing standard RJ45 jacks with miniaturized industrial SPE connectors (such as IEC 63171-6 or IEC 63171-1 interfaces) frees up critical edge space on the board. This allows hardware engineers to either drastically shrink the overall SBC form factor or allocate saved PCB area to additional processing units, dedicated security elements, or specialized sensor arrays.

Bill-of-Materials Reduction and Simplified Power Trees

Standard PoE implementations require complex magnetic modules, bridge rectifiers, and multi-stage power delivery controllers to negotiate and handle up to 48V–57V inputs across multiple wire pairs. In contrast, PoDL supports lower operating voltages tailored to embedded systems (such as 12V, 24V, or 48V regulated classes) over two conductors.

For low-voltage SBCs:

  • Fewer Discrete Components: PoDL coupling networks require fewer inductors and decoupling capacitors to separate the high-frequency data signal from the DC power supply.
  • Streamlined Regulation: Feeding lower voltage profiles directly into the SBC reduces the necessity for large, step-down buck converters, decreasing component count and overall manufacturing costs.
  • Unified Cabling: System integrators require only one lightweight, flexible cable for both operation and network communications.

Improved Thermal Profiles and Energy Efficiency

Power dissipation is a primary constraint in compact, fanless SBC designs. Large voltage drops from high-voltage standard PoE down to 3.3V or 5V core logic rails generate significant heat within enclosed casings.

PoDL addresses this through standardized power classes that deliver power optimized closer to the native input requirements of low-power processors and microcontrollers. Lower conversion overhead reduces localized hotspot generation on the PCB, mitigating thermal throttling and allowing processors to sustain peak performance in passively cooled or hermetically sealed enclosures.

Extended Reach for Industrial and Edge Deployments

The combination of PoDL with the 10BASE-T1L standard extends point-to-point network and power reach up to 1,000 meters, far exceeding the 100-meter limitation of standard 4-pair Ethernet.

Future low-voltage SBCs designed with 10BASE-T1L and PoDL can operate as long-distance edge gateways, remote sensor aggregators, and distributed field controllers in harsh environments without local auxiliary power supplies.

Future Design Considerations and Adoption

To fully realize PoDL integration, future SBC designs must overcome key hardware engineering considerations:

  1. Coupling Inductor Selection: Engineers must balance inductor size against saturation currents to prevent signal distortion at high operating temperatures.
  2. Standardization of Connectors: Adopting unified SPE connector standards is essential for interoperability across industrial and automotive ecosystems.
  3. PHY and Controller Integration: As semiconductor manufacturers release highly integrated SPE PHYs with built-in PoDL power management, SBC motherboards will achieve higher plug-and-play simplicity.

PoDL shifts low-voltage SBC development toward ultra-compact, highly reliable, and energy-dense systems, establishing single-pair connectivity as a foundational standard for the next generation of embedded computing.
