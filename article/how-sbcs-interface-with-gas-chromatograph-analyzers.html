How SBCs Interface with Gas Chromatograph Analyzers

Single board computers (SBCs) play a vital role in modern chemical plants by serving as compact, cost-effective data acquisition units, edge processors, and communication bridges for industrial gas chromatography (GC) analyzers. This article explores how SBCs connect to GC systems through physical I/O interfaces, handle real-time detector data, communicate via industrial protocols with Distributed Control Systems (DCS), and operate safely within harsh, hazardous plant environments.

Physical and Electrical Interfacing

Industrial gas chromatographs rely on sensitive detectors—such as Flame Ionization Detectors (FID), Thermal Conductivity Detectors (TCD), or Flame Photometric Detectors (FPD)—to measure chemical concentrations. SBCs interface with these analyzers using both analog and digital physical layers:

  • Analog-to-Digital Conversion (ADC): Raw analog outputs from GC detector electrometers are sampled using high-resolution (typically 24-bit) ADC expansion boards connected to the SBC via SPI or I2C buses. This ensures microvolt-level peak detection accuracy.
  • Industrial Serial Buses: Most modern GC analyzers feature onboard microcontrollers that output pre-processed data. SBCs interface with these controllers using integrated or HAT-based RS-232, RS-422, or isolated RS-485 serial ports to transmit instrument statuses and analytical results.
  • Industrial Ethernet: SBCs equipped with Gigabit Ethernet or dual-NIC configurations connect directly to the analyzer’s local network port for high-speed diagnostic data, raw chromatogram streaming, and remote firmware configuration.
  • General-Purpose I/O (GPIO) and Relays: SBC GPIO pins, coupled with optical isolation and industrial relay modules, manage hardware triggers such as stream switching valves, sample injection solenoids, and carrier gas pressure alarms.

Communication Protocols and System Integration

Once physical connectivity is established, SBCs translate proprietary or low-level instrument data into standardized industrial protocols, enabling seamless integration with plant-wide control architectures:

  • Modbus (RTU and TCP): SBCs commonly act as Modbus slaves or masters, exposing registers that hold component concentrations, retention times, peak areas, and alarm statuses to the facility's Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs) or DCS.
  • OPC UA: Running lightweight OPC UA servers on SBCs allows secure, platform-independent telemetry transmission directly to Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) platforms and enterprise asset management systems.
  • MQTT and REST APIs: For plants adopting Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) frameworks, SBCs publish analytical data over lightweight MQTT brokers to cloud analytics platforms for predictive maintenance and column degradation monitoring.

Edge Processing and Chromatogram Analysis

Rather than relying entirely on central plant servers to process chromatographic curves, SBCs perform real-time edge computing. Embedded software running on Linux-based real-time kernels (such as PREEMPT_RT) performs baseline drift correction, peak identification, peak integration, and calibration curve calculations directly at the analyzer shelter. This edge autonomy ensures that critical stream composition data is generated reliably even if the primary plant network experiences downtime.

Hazardous Area and Environmental Considerations

Chemical plants present demanding operating environments subject to strict safety regulations. Interfacing SBCs with gas chromatographs requires specific protective measures:

  • Intrinsic Safety and Galvanic Isolation: All electrical connections between the SBC, the GC, and external sensors must incorporate galvanic isolation barriers and optocouplers to prevent ground loops and voltage spikes.
  • Explosion-Proof Enclosures: When deployed in hazardous environments (such as ATEX Zone 1/Zone 2 or Class I, Division 1/Division 2 areas), SBCs are housed inside flameproof (Ex d) or purged/pressurized (Ex p) enclosures.
  • Industrial-Grade Hardware: Implementations utilize fanless, wide-temperature-rated (-40°C to +85°C) SBCs with conformal-coated circuit boards to resist corrosive atmospheric gases like hydrogen sulfide (\(H_2S\)) and chlorine.
