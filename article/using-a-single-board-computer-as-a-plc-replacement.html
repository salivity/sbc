Using a Single Board Computer as a PLC Replacement

Using a single-board computer (SBC) as a programmable logic controller (PLC) replacement offers cost savings, immense computational power, and flexible software options for modern industrial automation. However, transitioning from a hardened industrial PLC to a consumer or commercial-grade SBC requires addressing fundamental differences in hardware robustness, electrical standards, real-time operating capabilities, and long-term reliability. This article breaks down the essential technical, environmental, and software considerations required to successfully deploy an SBC in place of a traditional PLC.

Electrical Interfacing and Signal Conditioning

Traditional PLCs inherently support industrial voltage levels—most commonly 24V DC for digital inputs and outputs, as well as standard 0–10V or 4–20mA analog loops. Most SBCs operate strictly at 3.3V or 5V logic levels on their General Purpose Input/Output (GPIO) pins. Direct connection to industrial sensors or actuators will destroy the SBC. Replacing a PLC requires dedicated intermediate circuitry, including:

  • Optocouplers and Galvanic Isolation: Essential to isolate sensitive digital pins from high-voltage spikes, ground loops, and electrical noise.
  • Level Shifting and Drivers: Necessary to translate 3.3V/5V control signals up to 24V DC to trigger relays, solenoids, or motor starters.
  • Analog Signal Conditioning: External analog-to-digital converters (ADCs) and digital-to-analog converters (DACs) with built-in filtering and overvoltage protection are required, as many SBCs lack native, high-resolution analog inputs.

Determinism and Real-Time Execution

PLCs operate on deterministic scan cycles, ensuring tasks execute within precise timeframes. Standard SBC operating systems (like standard Linux or Android) are non-deterministic; background tasks, thread scheduling, and kernel interruptions introduce latency jitter that can compromise high-speed process control.

  • Real-Time Kernels: An SBC running Linux typically requires a real-time patch, such as PREEMPT_RT or Xenomai, to ensure low-latency and deterministic behavior.
  • Coprocessors: Some architectures offload time-critical tasks to integrated microcontrollers (like a dual-core system combining an ARM Cortex-A with a Cortex-M core) to maintain hard real-time execution.

Industrial Hardening and Environmental Tolerances

Factory floors present harsh operating environments that standard SBCs are not designed to endure. Environmental factors include:

  • Operating Temperatures: Commercial SBCs are often rated for 0°C to 50°C. Industrial PLCs handle -20°C to 60°C or broader. Industrial-grade SBC variants or active thermal management (heatsinks, rugged enclosures) must be used.
  • Electromagnetic Interference (EMI) and ESD: Heavy machinery causes transient voltage spikes and radio frequency interference. SBC enclosures must offer robust shielding, and power supplies must include transient voltage suppression (TVS) diodes and surge protection.
  • Vibration and Dust: Solid mounting (such as DIN-rail integration), conformally coated PCBs, and IP-rated enclosures protect components against physical shock, dust ingress, and moisture.

Storage and System Reliability

PLCs are designed for instant startup and immunity to sudden power losses. In contrast, common SBCs relying on micro-SD cards frequently suffer file-system corruption if power is removed unexpectedly during a write cycle.

  • Storage Media: Utilize onboard eMMC storage, NVMe drives, or read-only file systems to prevent data corruption.
  • Watchdog Timers: Hardware watchdog timers should be implemented to automatically reboot or safely fault the system if the main OS hangs.
  • Power Loss Recovery: Adding a supercapacitor-based uninterruptible power supply (UPS) allows the SBC to perform a clean shutdown during sudden power outages.

Software Stack and Maintenance

While SBCs allow programmers to write automation logic in C++, Python, or Rust, standard industrial environments rely heavily on IEC 61131-3 programming languages (Ladder Logic, Structured Text, Function Block Diagrams) for troubleshooting by plant technicians.

  • PLC Runtimes: Open-source platforms like OpenPLC or commercial runtimes like CODESYS can be deployed on Linux-based SBCs to provide IEC 61131-3 compatibility.
  • Security and Updates: A network-connected SBC requires an active management strategy for security patches, firewall configurations, and long-term OS updates without interrupting ongoing production.

Safety and Certification Requirements

PLCs often carry critical certifications (such as UL, CE, and functional safety ratings like SIL 2 or SIL 3) that guarantee fail-safe behaviors. SBC-based custom systems rarely have these certifications out of the box. For critical safety loops—such as emergency stops and personnel safety interlocks—dedicated, certified safety relays or safety PLCs should remain in place rather than relying solely on a software-driven SBC.
