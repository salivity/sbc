USB Differential Signaling on Single Board Computers

This article provides an overview of the electrical characteristics governing differential signaling on the USB lines of single board computers (SBCs). It examines physical layer parameters across various USB standards, detailing differential voltage levels, characteristic impedance, signal termination, receiver thresholds, and board-level routing requirements critical for robust hardware design.

Differential Voltage Swings Across USB Modes

USB communication on single board computers relies on balanced differential pairs—designated as \(D+\) and \(D-\) for USB 2.0 and lower, or dedicated SuperSpeed differential transmitter/receiver pairs (SSTX/SSRX) for USB 3.x. The electrical amplitude varies significantly depending on the operating mode:

  • Low-Speed (1.5 Mbps) and Full-Speed (12 Mbps): These modes use voltage-mode signaling with nominal 3.3V logic levels. A differential '1' occurs when \(D+\) exceeds 2.8V and \(D-\) is below 0.3V. A differential '0' occurs when \(D-\) exceeds 2.8V and \(D+\) is below 0.3V. The nominal single-ended voltage swing is 0V to 3.3V, resulting in a large differential swing of approximately \(\pm 3.0\text{V}\).
  • High-Speed (480 Mbps): High-speed USB switches from voltage-mode to current-mode signaling to reduce EMI and power consumption. The transceiver drives a constant current of \(17.78\text{ mA}\) into a \(45\ \Omega\) load, creating a nominal differential voltage swing of \(\pm 400\text{ mV}\) (a \(800\text{ mV}\) peak-to-peak differential amplitude). A logic high corresponds to \(D+\) at \(+400\text{ mV}\) relative to \(D-\), while a logic low inverts this relationship.
  • SuperSpeed (5 Gbps and above): USB 3.x utilizes low-voltage differential signaling (LVDS) with AC-coupling capacitors (typically \(75\text{ nF}\) to \(200\text{ nF}\)) on the transmit lines. The differential peak-to-peak output voltage typically ranges between \(800\text{ mV}\) and \(1200\text{ mV}\), with deemphasis techniques applied to compensate for high-frequency channel losses.

Impedance Matching and Termination

To avoid signal reflections and ensure maximum power transfer, strict impedance controls must be maintained from the SBC system-on-chip (SoC) or USB hub controller through the PCB traces to the physical connector:

  • Differential Impedance (\(Z_{\text{diff}}\)): Must be precisely controlled to \(90\ \Omega \pm 10\%\).
  • Single-Ended Characteristic Impedance (\(Z_0\)): Each line of the differential pair must exhibit \(45\ \Omega \pm 10\%\) relative to the reference ground plane.
  • Termination Resistors: High-speed transceivers include internal \(45\ \Omega\) terminations pulled down to ground on both \(D+\) and \(D-\), providing the required \(90\ \Omega\) parallel differential termination. Low/Full-speed modes utilize pull-up resistors (\(1.5\ \text{k}\Omega\)) on either \(D+\) (Full-Speed) or \(D-\) (Low-Speed) to signal device attachment and speed enumeration.

Receiver Sensitivity and Thresholds

SBC USB physical layer (PHY) receivers must detect signals accurately in the presence of noise and attenuation:

  • High-Speed Receiver Sensitivity: The high-speed receiver must detect differential signals with an amplitude as low as \(\pm 100\text{ mV}\).
  • Squelch Threshold: Signals below \(\pm 100\text{ mV}\) differential are ignored (squelched) to prevent false packet triggers from line noise. The squelch detector is disabled once valid data exceeding \(\pm 150\text{ mV}\) is detected.
  • Disconnect Threshold: A high-speed disconnect state is detected when the differential amplitude exceeds approximately \(525\text{ mV}\) to \(625\text{ mV}\) due to the doubling of impedance when the load is removed.

PCB Layout and Parasitic Constraints on SBCs

Single board computers operate in compact form factors, making differential signal integrity highly sensitive to layout design:

  • Trace Length Matching (Intra-pair Skew): The physical length difference between \(D+\) and \(D-\) (or SSTX+/SSTX-) should be kept under \(0.15\text{ mm}\) (approx. \(5\text{ ps}\) delay) to prevent common-mode noise conversion and jitter.
  • Capacitance Limits: External components placed on the lines, such as electrostatic discharge (ESD) protection diodes, must feature ultra-low junction capacitance—typically under \(0.5\text{ pF}\) for High-Speed and under \(0.2\text{ pF}\) for SuperSpeed—to avoid degrading signal rise/fall times.
  • Continuous Reference Plane: Differential traces must be routed over an unbroken ground reference plane without crossing plane splits to maintain continuous characteristic impedance.
