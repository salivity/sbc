Modern 40-Pin SBC GPIO Header Pinout Guide

Most modern Single Board Computers (SBCs)—such as the Raspberry Pi, Orange Pi, Radxa Rock Pi, and ASUS Tinker Board—utilize a standardized 40-pin GPIO (General Purpose Input/Output) header. This article provides a direct overview of the standard 40-pin dual-row layout, detailing power distribution, dedicated communication buses (I2C, SPI, UART), hardware PWM channels, and general digital I/O lines shared across contemporary SBC platforms.

The Standard Physical Layout

The modern 40-pin layout consists of a 2x20 male header with a standard 2.54mm (0.1 inch) pin pitch. Odd-numbered pins occupy the inner/bottom row, while even-numbered pins occupy the outer/top row. Almost all modern 40-pin SBCs adhere to 3.3V logic levels for digital signals, and pins are typically not 5V-tolerant unless explicitly stated by the manufacturer.

Power and Ground Distribution

Power pins provide fixed rail voltages directly from the board's power management circuitry:

  • 5V Power: Pin 2, Pin 4
  • 3.3V Power: Pin 1, Pin 17
  • Ground (GND): Pin 6, Pin 9, Pin 14, Pin 20, Pin 25, Pin 30, Pin 34, Pin 39

Primary Communication Interfaces

Modern SBCs multiplex several hardware communication buses across specific physical pins:

1. I2C (Inter-Integrated Circuit)

  • I2C SDA (Data): Pin 3 (GPIO 2)
  • I2C SCL (Clock): Pin 5 (GPIO 3)
  • HAT / Identification I2C: Pin 27 (ID_SD) and Pin 28 (ID_SC) are reserved for board identification and auto-configuration EEPROMs.

2. UART (Universal Asynchronous Receiver-Transmitter)

  • UART TXD (Transmit): Pin 8 (GPIO 14)
  • UART RXD (Receive): Pin 10 (GPIO 15)

3. SPI (Serial Peripheral Interface)

  • SPI0 MOSI (Master Out Slave In): Pin 19 (GPIO 10)
  • SPI0 MISO (Master In Slave Out): Pin 21 (GPIO 9)
  • SPI0 SCLK (Serial Clock): Pin 23 (GPIO 11)
  • SPI0 CE0 (Chip Enable 0): Pin 24 (GPIO 8)
  • SPI0 CE1 (Chip Enable 1): Pin 26 (GPIO 7)

Hardware PWM and Digital GPIO

The remaining pins function primarily as general-purpose digital input/output lines, with several supporting hardware Pulse Width Modulation (PWM):

  • PWM Channels: Commonly mapped to Pin 12 (GPIO 18), Pin 32 (GPIO 12), Pin 33 (GPIO 13), and Pin 35 (GPIO 19).
  • Additional Digital GPIOs:
    • Pin 7 (GPIO 4)
    • Pin 11 (GPIO 17)
    • Pin 13 (GPIO 27)
    • Pin 15 (GPIO 22)
    • Pin 16 (GPIO 23)
    • Pin 18 (GPIO 24)
    • Pin 22 (GPIO 25)
    • Pin 29 (GPIO 5)
    • Pin 31 (GPIO 6)
    • Pin 36 (GPIO 16)
    • Pin 37 (GPIO 26)
    • Pin 38 (GPIO 20)
    • Pin 40 (GPIO 21)

Cross-Platform Compatibility Considerations

While physical pin placement and default primary functions (I2C, SPI, UART, Power) are largely identical across SBCs to ensure HAT and accessory compatibility, internal pin multiplexing, SoC register addresses, pull-up/pull-down resistor values, and pin-naming schemes (e.g., Broadcom BCM numbering versus Linux GPIO sysfs/libgpiod numbers) vary depending on the underlying processor architecture (Broadcom, Rockchip, Allwinner, or Amlogic).
