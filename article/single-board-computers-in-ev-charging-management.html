Single Board Computers in EV Charging Management

Single board computers (SBCs) act as the central intelligence in localized electric vehicle (EV) charging stations, handling critical operations ranging from power regulation to network communications. By combining processing power, input/output interfaces, and connectivity on a single compact board, SBCs enable charging stations to manage local energy distribution, enforce communication standards like OCPP, authenticate users, and maintain operational stability even when disconnected from central networks.

Core System Control and Hardware Interfacing

At the fundamental level, an SBC serves as the primary controller for the charging station's physical hardware. It interfaces directly with power electronics, safety relays, and sensing units via general-purpose input/output (GPIO) pins, serial buses (such as SPI and I2C), and Controller Area Network (CAN) interfaces.

The SBC continuously monitors critical parameters, including voltage levels, current draw, and internal temperatures. If an anomaly occurs—such as a ground fault, short circuit, or overheating—the SBC instantly triggers safety mechanisms and disconnects the power supply to protect both the vehicle and the local electrical infrastructure.

Standardized Communication Protocols

Localized charging points must adhere to industry-standard protocols to remain interoperable. SBCs run lightweight operating systems (typically Linux-based) capable of executing complex software stacks, including:

  • Open Charge Point Protocol (OCPP): Enables standardized communication between the charge point and the central Charging Station Management System (CSMS) for billing, status reporting, and remote diagnostics.
  • ISO 15118 and IEC 61851: Facilitates advanced vehicle-to-grid (V2G) communication and features like "Plug & Charge," where the vehicle automatically authenticates and authorizes charging upon connection.

Local Load Management and Grid Balancing

In localized environments—such as residential complexes, commercial parking garages, or fleet depots—multiple chargers often share a limited power supply. SBCs enable dynamic load management (DLM) at the edge without requiring constant cloud connectivity.

By analyzing real-time capacity and the energy demands of connected vehicles, the SBC dynamically throttles or increases the charging rate of individual units. This localized intelligence prevents electrical overloads, eliminates peak-demand utility penalties, and seamlessly coordinates with on-site renewable energy sources, such as solar arrays and battery energy storage systems (BESS).

User Authentication and Payment Processing

SBCs manage peripheral devices required for end-user interaction. They process inputs from:

  • RFID/NFC readers for member badge verification.
  • Touchscreen displays and digital payment terminals.
  • QR code scanners and mobile app integrations over Bluetooth or local Wi-Fi.

Because the SBC processes these transactions locally before securely transmitting data upstream, the authorization process is fast and minimizes latency for the user.

Edge Computing and Offline Resilience

Reliability is critical for EV infrastructure. Relying solely on cloud connectivity can lead to station downtime during network outages. An SBC provides sufficient computing power and local storage to run edge diagnostics, cache transaction logs, and continue standard charging operations autonomously when offline. Once the network connection is restored, the SBC automatically syncs data back to the central server, ensuring uninterrupted service and accurate billing.
