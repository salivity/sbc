Milk-V Mars CM Legacy Carrier Board Compatibility

The Milk-V Mars CM is a compact system-on-module powered by the StarFive JH7110 RISC-V processor, designed specifically to match the Raspberry Pi Compute Module 4 (CM4) form factor. This article explains how the Milk-V Mars CM interfaces with legacy CM4 carrier boards, highlighting its mechanical layout, electrical pin compatibility, standard peripheral interfaces, and necessary software considerations for seamless integration.

Form Factor and Mechanical Alignment

The Milk-V Mars CM utilizes the exact physical footprint of the standard CM4 ecosystem, measuring 55 mm × 40 mm with identical mounting hole placements. It connects to carrier boards via dual 100-pin high-density board-to-board mezzanine connectors on the bottom of the module. This standardized layout ensures that legacy baseboards, heatsinks, and enclosures designed for the CM4 can physically host the Mars CM without requiring structural modifications.

Electrical Interface and Pinout Mapping

The Mars CM routes its signals through the dual 100-pin connectors to closely mirror the standard CM4 pin definitions. Key electrical interfaces include:

  • Power Supply: The module accepts a standard 5V DC input from the carrier board, utilizing an onboard power management integrated circuit (PMIC) to generate the lower voltages required by the JH7110 SoC and LPDDR4 memory.
  • PCIe Interface: It provides a PCIe 2.0 x1 lane, enabling direct communication with NVMe storage, network cards, or expansion switches routed on the carrier board.
  • Networking: The module includes an onboard Gigabit Ethernet PHY, delivering RGMII/Ethernet signals directly to the carrier board’s RJ45 magnetic jack.
  • Display and Camera: Native support for HDMI 2.0 output, MIPI DSI (display serial interface), and MIPI CSI (camera serial interface) matches the standard signal routing on existing baseboards.
  • USB and GPIO: It outputs USB 2.0 and USB 3.0 lines along with general-purpose I/O (GPIO) pins, supporting UART, I2C, SPI, and PWM peripherals.

Signal Multiplexing and Functional Nuances

While the primary interfaces align with the CM4 standard, differences exist due to the architectural divergence between Broadcom and StarFive SoCs. Certain alternate pin functions, analog-to-digital converter (ADC) channels, or proprietary GPIO multiplexing configurations on specialized carrier boards may not map identically. For standard breakout boards featuring USB, Ethernet, HDMI, and standard 40-pin headers, the Mars CM functions as a drop-in hardware replacement.

Software and Device Tree Configuration

Hardware compatibility allows the Mars CM to power on and communicate over physical traces, but legacy software images built for ARM architectures will not run on the module. Interfacing successfully requires:

  1. RISC-V OS Images: Booting a compatible 64-bit RISC-V Linux distribution (such as Debian, Ubuntu, or Fedora compiled for RV64GC).
  2. Device Tree Customization: Using a Device Tree Blob (DTB) tailored to both the Milk-V Mars CM and the specific peripheral layout of the carrier board to initialize drivers for PCIe controllers, Ethernet PHYs, and external expansion chips correctly.
