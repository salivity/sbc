Radxa Rock 3A: RK3568 SoC and M.2 Expansion

The Radxa Rock 3A combines the Rockchip RK3568 system-on-chip (SoC) with versatile M.2 expansion interfaces, creating a high-performance single-board computer (SBC) for edge computing, multimedia, and network storage. This article explores how the board leverages the internal architecture and flexible I/O lanes of the RK3568 to provide native M.2 storage and wireless expansion directly on a compact form factor.

RK3568 Native I/O and Processing Core

At the core of the Radxa Rock 3A is the Rockchip RK3568, a 22nm processor featuring a quad-core 64-bit Arm Cortex-A55 CPU running up to 2.0 GHz, an Arm Mali-G52 GPU, and an integrated 0.8 TOPS Neural Processing Unit (NPU). Beyond general-purpose computing and machine learning acceleration, the key architectural strength of the RK3568 lies in its advanced SerDes (Serializer/Deserializer) lane multiplexing. The chip natively provides built-in PCIe 3.0 and PCIe 2.1 controllers, eliminating the need for external bridge chips that typically introduce latency and reduce bandwidth on smaller development boards.

M.2 M-Key for High-Speed NVMe Storage

The Radxa Rock 3A routes the RK3568 processor's PCIe 3.0 lanes to an onboard M.2 M-Key connector located on the underside of the board. This connection delivers PCIe 3.0 x2 bandwidth (up to 16 Gbps / ~2 GB/s theoretical throughput).

By connecting directly to the SoC's PCIe root complex, the M.2 M-Key interface supports standard NVMe 2280 or 2230 solid-state drives (SSDs). This direct integration bypasses the performance bottlenecks typical of eMMC, MicroSD, or USB-bridged storage, making the board capable of serving as a network-attached storage (NAS) device, database server, or fast-boot edge gateway. Additionally, the RK3568's SATA multiplexing allows the interface to support SATA-based M.2 drives through software configuration.

M.2 E-Key for Wireless Connectivity

To manage communication without consuming USB 3.0 or Ethernet resources, the Rock 3A includes a dedicated M.2 E-Key slot on its top layer. This slot provides connections for PCIe and SDIO interfaces routed directly from the SoC.

The M.2 E-Key interface is engineered primarily for wireless expansion, supporting modern Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax) and Bluetooth 5.x modules. Because the data transfers across dedicated high-speed buses rather than shared USB controllers, wireless networking achieves lower latency, reduced CPU overhead, and higher sustained throughput.

System-Level Performance and Integration Benefits

The integration of the RK3568 and M.2 expansion yields several structural and functional advantages:

  • Elimination of I/O Bottlenecks: Direct PCIe routing reduces latency and allows simultaneous full-speed operation of Ethernet, USB 3.0, and M.2 storage without bandwidth contention.
  • Thermal and Power Efficiency: The energy-efficient Cortex-A55 cores and native power management of the RK3568 maintain stability during continuous high-speed reads and writes to connected M.2 devices.
  • Compact Form Factor: By placing the M.2 slots directly on the PCB (utilizing both the top and bottom surfaces), the Rock 3A maintains a standard 85 x 54 mm footprint while offering expansion capabilities typically found on larger mini-ITX or industrial carrier boards.
