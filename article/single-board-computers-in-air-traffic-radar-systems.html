Single Board Computers in Air Traffic Radar Systems

Single-board computers (SBCs) are vital components in modern localized air traffic tracking radar stations, offering cost-effective, low-power, and compact processing capabilities for flight telemetry. These devices act as the primary edge-computing hub, interfacing directly with radio frequency receivers, decoding transponder signals, and relaying real-time aircraft positioning data to local monitors and global aviation networks.

Signal Ingestion and Demodulation

Localized radar stations typically utilize Software Defined Radios (SDRs) plugged into an SBC's USB or general-purpose input/output (GPIO) ports. The SBC powers the SDR and continuously captures radio frequencies used in aviation transponders, such as 1090 MHz for Automatic Dependent Surveillance-Broadcast (ADS-B) and Mode S, or 978 MHz for Universal Access Transceivers (UAT). The processing core of the SBC runs demodulation software that translates raw analog radio waves into structured binary packets.

Edge Data Processing and Decoding

Once the raw signals are captured, the SBC decodes the data packets to extract critical flight parameters, including ICAO aircraft identifiers, flight numbers, altitude, ground speed, heading, and GPS coordinates. By handling this computational workload at the antenna site, the SBC eliminates the need for expensive high-end servers. It filters out corrupted packets, calculates signal strength metrics, and formats the data into standard protocols such as ASTERIX or JSON for downstream consumption.

Network Communication and Data Distribution

The SBC serves as the network bridge for the localized radar station. Utilizing onboard Ethernet, Wi-Fi, or cellular modems, it distributes processed aircraft data to multiple destinations simultaneously:

  • Local Displays: Providing low-latency situational awareness feeds to private airfields, drone operators, or localized air traffic management screens.
  • Flight Tracking Networks: Uploading encrypted telemetry feeds to global aggregation services and aviation safety repositories.
  • Alerting Systems: Triggering automated proximity alerts or airspace violation warnings locally.

Remote and Off-Grid Station Management

Air traffic tracking receivers are frequently installed on high masts, rooftops, or remote elevated terrain to achieve unobstructed line-of-sight with aircraft. The minimal power requirements of SBCs enable them to run continuously on solar panels paired with battery storage systems. Furthermore, their Linux-based operating systems support secure remote access via SSH or VPNs, allowing technicians to deploy software updates, monitor system health, and adjust radio gains without physical site visits.
