Raspberry Pi Zero vs Pi 4 Size Comparison

Choosing the right Raspberry Pi for your project often comes down to balancing computational power with physical constraints. This article provides a direct size and form factor comparison between the ultra-compact Raspberry Pi Zero series and the full-sized Raspberry Pi 4 Model B. While the Raspberry Pi 4 offers robust desktop-level performance, it demands a much larger footprint, making the diminutive Raspberry Pi Zero the go-to choice for space-constrained and low-profile embedded applications.

Dimensions and Footprint

The physical differences between these two boards are immediately apparent when looking at their dimensions. The Raspberry Pi Zero is designed with a "gumstick" form factor, prioritizing minimalism, whereas the Raspberry Pi 4 uses the standard "credit card" footprint established by earlier flagship Pi models.

  • Raspberry Pi Zero (and Zero W / Zero 2 W): Measures 65 mm × 30 mm (2.56" × 1.18"), with a board thickness of approximately 5 mm.
  • Raspberry Pi 4 Model B: Measures 85 mm × 56 mm (3.35" × 2.20"), with a total height of about 16 mm due to the stacked USB and Ethernet ports.

In terms of total surface area, the Raspberry Pi Zero occupies less than 42% of the space required by a Raspberry Pi 4.

Weight and Thickness Comparison

For projects like drones, wearable tech, or compact IoT sensors, weight and vertical clearance are just as critical as length and width.

  • Weight: The Raspberry Pi Zero weighs a mere 9 grams (approx. 0.3 oz). In contrast, the Raspberry Pi 4 weighs roughly 46 grams (approx. 1.6 oz) because of its heavier, multi-layered PCB and bulky metal ports.
  • Vertical Profile: The Pi Zero is incredibly flat because it uses micro-ports (Mini HDMI and Micro-USB) and leaves its 40-pin GPIO header unpopulated by default. The Pi 4 features full-sized USB Type-A ports, an Ethernet jack, and a USB-C power port, making it more than three times taller than the Zero.

Port Layout and Spatial Impact

The physical size of the board itself is only part of the story; the cables and connectors you plug into it also impact the final footprint of your project.

Raspberry Pi 4 Connectivity

The Pi 4 is a connectivity powerhouse, featuring two USB 3.0 ports, two USB 2.0 ports, a Gigabit Ethernet jack, two Micro-HDMI ports, and a USB-C power input. Because these ports project outward from multiple sides of the board, a fully connected Pi 4 requires significant breathing room inside an enclosure.

Raspberry Pi Zero Connectivity

To maintain its tiny profile, the Pi Zero strips away all bulky interfaces. It features a single Micro-USB data port, a Micro-USB power port, and a single Mini-HDMI output. Users often need adapters or hubs to connect standard USB devices, which can ultimately expand the project's footprint beyond the board's native 65 mm length.
