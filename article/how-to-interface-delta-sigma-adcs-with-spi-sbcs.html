How to Interface Delta-Sigma ADCs with SPI SBCs

Interfacing high-precision delta-sigma (\(\Delta\Sigma\)) analog-to-digital converters (ADCs) with a Serial Peripheral Interface (SPI)-capable single-board computer (SBC) requires a coordinated approach combining hardware wiring, precise bus timing, interrupt-driven data retrieval, and signal conditioning. This guide outlines the essential steps developers take to successfully connect these sensitive converters to SBCs (such as a Raspberry Pi, BeagleBone, or Jetson), configure digital communication registers, capture high-resolution samples without data corruption, and minimize digital switching noise.

1. Hardware Connections and Pin Configuration

Connecting a delta-sigma ADC to an SBC requires standard SPI lines alongside specialized control pins:

  • SCLK (Serial Clock): Generated by the SBC master to clock data in and out.
  • MOSI / DIN (Master Out Slave In): Transmits configuration registers, gain settings, and channel multiplexer commands from the SBC to the ADC.
  • MISO / DOUT (Master In Slave Out): Carries the converted digital sample back to the SBC.
  • CS / SS (Chip Select): Enables the specific ADC on the shared SPI bus.
  • \(\overline{\text{DRDY}}\) (Data Ready): A critical dedicated output pin on delta-sigma ADCs that asserts low whenever a new conversion is complete. This pin must connect to an interrupt-capable GPIO pin on the SBC.
  • RESET and PWDN: Optional GPIO connections to hardware-reset or power-down the ADC.

2. Configuring SPI Modes and Bus Speeds

Delta-sigma ADCs are sensitive to clock phase and polarity. Developers must consult the device datasheet to select the exact SPI mode:

  • SPI Mode: Most precision ADCs (such as the ADS1256 or AD717x families) operate under SPI Mode 1 (CPOL = 0, CPHA = 1) or SPI Mode 3 (CPOL = 1, CPHA = 1), where data is latched on the falling or rising edge depending on the specific architecture.
  • Clock Frequency: The maximum SPI clock rate is often constrained by internal digital filter conversion periods or interface limitations. While an SBC can generate SPI clocks above 20 MHz, precision ADCs typically require lower frequencies (e.g., 1 MHz to 8 MHz) to prevent timing violations.

3. Implementing the Data-Ready Interrupt Loop

Unlike standard successive-approximation (SAR) ADCs that convert on-demand, delta-sigma converters produce samples continuously according to their internal modulator and digital decimation filter.

  1. Avoid Polling: Continuously polling the SPI bus wastes CPU resources and injects high-frequency bus noise into the converter during sensitive analog integration cycles.
  2. Use Hardware Interrupts: Configure the SBC operating system (e.g., Linux GPIO interrupts via libgpiod or kernel drivers) to trigger on the falling edge of the \(\overline{\text{DRDY}}\) line.
  3. Read Synchronization: When the interrupt triggers, the SBC asserts Chip Select (CS low), sends the "Read Data" command if required by the protocol, clocks in the multi-byte sample (typically 24-bit or 32-bit two's complement integers), and de-asserts CS before the next conversion cycle finishes.

4. Software Driver and Data Conversion

At the software layer, developers initialize the converter using a structured sequence:

  1. Register Initialization: Send SPI write commands to configure the internal programmable gain amplifier (PGA), input multiplexer (single-ended vs. differential), internal reference voltage, and output data rate (ODR).
  2. Offset and Gain Calibration: Trigger internal or system zero-scale and full-scale self-calibration routines through control register commands.
  3. Sample Processing: Read the incoming 24-bit or 32-bit raw integer over SPI, sign-extend the value to a standard 32-bit or 64-bit integer, and convert it to absolute voltage using the formula: \[\text{Voltage} = \frac{\text{Raw Code} \times V_{\text{REF}}}{\text{Gain} \times (2^{N-1} - 1)}\] where \(N\) represents the ADC resolution in bits.

5. Layout and Noise Mitigation Strategies

High-precision delta-sigma ADCs can detect changes down to microvolts or nanovolts. To prevent the high-speed SBC digital electronics from degrading measurement accuracy:

  • Ground Plane Integrity: Use a solid, low-impedance ground plane. Route SPI traces away from sensitive analog input signals.
  • Series Damping Resistors: Place 22 \(\Omega\) to 47 \(\Omega\) series resistors on SCLK, MOSI, and MISO traces near the SBC to slow down digital edge transitions (ringing) and reduce radiated EMI.
  • Galvanic Isolation: For maximum precision or industrial environments, insert a multi-channel digital isolator (such as an ADuM or ISO77xx series IC) between the SBC SPI pins and the ADC to completely separate noisy processor grounds from clean analog measurement grounds.
