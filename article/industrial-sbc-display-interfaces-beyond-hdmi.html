Industrial SBC Display Interfaces Beyond HDMI

While HDMI and standard DisplayPort are the most common interfaces for external consumer monitors, industrial single board computers (SBCs) must support a diverse range of internal, low-power, and legacy display standards. Industrial applications frequently require direct integration with bare LCD panels, rugged touchscreens, and legacy human-machine interfaces (HMIs). To meet these needs, industrial SBCs commonly feature technologies such as LVDS, eDP, MIPI DSI, Parallel RGB, and legacy analog VGA.

Low-Voltage Differential Signaling (LVDS)

LVDS is one of the most widely adopted internal display interfaces in industrial computing. It uses differential signaling to transmit high-speed digital data over copper cables with low power consumption and high noise immunity.

  • Key Benefits: High resistance to electromagnetic interference (EMI), long-term availability, and the ability to drive displays over relatively long internal cable runs (up to several meters).
  • Common Use Cases: Rugged HMIs, medical devices, kiosk systems, and factory automation panels.

Embedded DisplayPort (eDP)

eDP is the modern successor to LVDS, based on the VESA DisplayPort standard but designed specifically for internal display connections.

  • Key Benefits: Higher data transfer rates, reduced pin count, lower power consumption, and native support for higher resolutions (such as 4K and beyond) with high refresh rates.
  • Common Use Cases: Modern industrial tablets, high-resolution medical imaging devices, and advanced digital signage.

MIPI Display Serial Interface (MIPI DSI)

Originally developed for the mobile industry, MIPI DSI is increasingly prevalent on ARM-based and compact x86 industrial SBCs. It is a high-speed serial interface designed for point-to-point connections between a processor and a display module.

  • Key Benefits: Extremely low power consumption, minimal PCB footprint, and direct integration with small-to-medium-sized LCD and OLED screens.
  • Common Use Cases: Handheld industrial diagnostic tools, smart meters, wearable devices, and compact IoT edge controllers.

Parallel RGB (TTL)

Parallel RGB, often referred to as TTL (Transistor-Transistor Logic), sends pixel data concurrently across multiple parallel data lines along with clock and synchronization signals.

  • Key Benefits: Simple design with no complex serialization hardware required on the display module, making display panels cost-effective.
  • Limitations: Prone to EMI and limited to short trace lengths and lower resolutions (typically up to WVGA or SVGA).
  • Common Use Cases: Cost-sensitive embedded systems, small system status screens, and simple instrumentation.

Analog RGB / VGA

Despite being phased out of consumer electronics, analog VGA remains a staple on many industrial SBCs to maintain backward compatibility with legacy infrastructure.

  • Key Benefits: Plug-and-play compatibility with older CRT and LCD monitors without requiring active signal converters.
  • Common Use Cases: Legacy factory control rooms, marine monitoring equipment, and legacy machinery retrofits.

Serial Interfaces (SPI and I2C)

For minimal display requirements, many industrial SBCs use low-speed serial buses like SPI (Serial Peripheral Interface) or I2C.

  • Key Benefits: Uses very few GPIO pins and requires minimal processing overhead.
  • Common Use Cases: Small monochrome displays, character LCDs, and auxiliary status OLED screens used for system diagnostics.
