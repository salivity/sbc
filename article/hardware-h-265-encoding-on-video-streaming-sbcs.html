Hardware H.265 Encoding on Video Streaming SBCs

Hardware-accelerated H.265 (HEVC) encoding allows single board computers (SBCs) to process and transmit high-definition video efficiently by shifting computationally heavy compression tasks from the central processor to specialized onboard silicon. This integration provides video-streaming SBCs with superior data compression, reduced CPU workloads, lower thermal outputs, and minimal latency, making them highly capable platforms for modern live-streaming and edge-computing applications.

50% Greater Compression Efficiency

H.265 provides approximately twice the data compression ratio of its predecessor, H.264, while maintaining the same level of visual quality. For an SBC streaming video over bandwidth-constrained networks—such as cellular data or Wi-Fi—this efficiency cuts the required upload bitrate in half. Consequently, users experience smoother streaming, fewer dropped frames, and significant reductions in cloud storage or transmission costs.

Preserved CPU Resources

Software-based video encoding is mathematically intensive and can easily push an SBC's general-purpose CPU cores to 100% utilization. Hardware acceleration utilizes a dedicated Video Processing Unit (VPU) built into the system-on-chip (SoC). Offloading the encoding process to this dedicated hardware leaves the CPU free to handle other critical operations, such as running the web server, executing computer vision algorithms, encrypting streams, or managing audio input.

Lower Power Consumption and Thermal Stability

SBCs are frequently deployed in compact, fanless enclosures or powered by batteries in remote environments. General-purpose CPU encoding generates high heat and drains power rapidly, often leading to thermal throttling and system instability. Hardware encoding blocks are hardwired specifically for video algorithms, allowing them to encode frames using a fraction of the electrical power and generating substantially less heat.

Ultra-Low Latency for Live Feeds

Real-time streaming applications, such as security feeds, drone FPV (First Person View), and interactive web broadcasting, require minimal delay between capture and playback. Hardware encoders process raw frames directly from camera interfaces (like MIPI-CSI or USB) in a fixed-function pipeline, delivering consistent, sub-frame encoding times that prevent the buffering delays inherent to software rendering.

Native 4K and High-Framerate Capability

Most budget and mid-tier SBC processors lack the computational power to encode 1080p60 or 4K video using raw CPU execution. A dedicated H.265 hardware encoder enables even low-cost single board computers to natively stream high-resolution, high-framerate content reliably without dropping frames or freezing the operating system.
