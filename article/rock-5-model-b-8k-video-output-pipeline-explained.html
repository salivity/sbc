Rock 5 Model B 8K Video Output Pipeline Explained

The Rock 5 Model B single-board computer achieves ultra-high-definition 8K video output through the advanced multi-display architecture of the Rockchip RK3588 system on a chip (SoC). Powered by a dedicated hardware video decoding unit (VPU) and the second-generation Video Output Processor (VOP2), the board routes high-bandwidth 8K video streams directly to high-speed physical display controllers, notably HDMI 2.1 and DisplayPort over USB Type-C.

The RK3588 Video Processing Unit (VPU)

At the start of the 8K pipeline is the hardware VPU, which decodes demanding codecs including 8K@60fps H.265, VP9, and AVS2, as well as 8K@30fps H.264. By offloading decoding entirely from the octa-core CPU to specialized silicon, the board minimizes latency and power consumption while processing up to 7680x4320 resolution streams without dropped frames.

The VOP2 Display Engine

Once decoded or rendered by the Mali-G610 GPU, pixel data is managed by the RK3588's Video Output Processor (VOP2). The VOP2 features multiple dedicated hardware planes and overlay layers:

  • Cluster Layers: High-performance layers dedicated to 4K and 8K primary surfaces with hardware color-space conversion, scaling, and rotation.
  • Esmart Layers: Secondary layers used for overlays, user interfaces, or lower-resolution auxiliary displays.

The VOP2 combines, scales, and maps these layers into dedicated display paths before dispatching the final image data to the output PHYs (physical layer controllers).

Display Interfaces and Bandwidth Routing

The Rock 5 Model B features several physical display outputs, but 8K output is restricted to specific interfaces due to bandwidth constraints:

  1. HDMI 2.1 Output (Port A): The primary 8K channel supports up to 8K@60Hz (with DSC or 4:2:0 chroma subsampling) or uncompressed 8K@30Hz. It utilizes fixed rate link (FRL) signaling to achieve the necessary bandwidth (up to 48 Gbps PHY rate).
  2. USB Type-C (DP 1.4 Alt Mode): The secondary 8K capable interface outputs DisplayPort 1.4 signals, supporting up to 8K@30Hz via HBR3 (High Bit Rate 3) lane configurations.
  3. HDMI 2.0 (Port B) and MIPI DSI: These additional interfaces handle up to 4K@60Hz and are driven by separate sub-pipelines within the VOP2.

Multi-Display Resource Allocation

The VOP2 dynamically allocates hardware channels based on active displays. When driving an 8K display on the primary HDMI 2.1 port, the SoC assigns its maximum pipeline bandwidth and primary cluster planes to that output. The remaining hardware layers can simultaneously drive additional displays (such as 4K screens over the second HDMI port or MIPI DSI), allowing the Rock 5 Model B to maintain independent multi-screen configurations without sacrificing the 8K resolution on the primary display.
