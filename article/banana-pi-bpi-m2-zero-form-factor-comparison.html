Banana Pi BPI-M2 Zero Form Factor Comparison

The Banana Pi BPI-M2 Zero is an ultra-compact single-board computer (SBC) designed to match the minimalist footprint popularized by micro-computing platforms. This article examines the physical dimensions, connector layout, and overall design of the BPI-M2 Zero, comparing its form factor directly against prominent ultra-compact boards such as the Raspberry Pi Zero series, the Orange Pi Zero family, and modern alternatives like the Radxa Zero.

Dimensions and Weight

The Banana Pi BPI-M2 Zero measures exactly 65 mm × 30 mm (2.56 in × 1.18 in) and weighs approximately 15 grams. This standard "gumstick" design aligns with the most common ultra-compact specifications in the maker industry.

  • Length: 65 mm
  • Width: 30 mm
  • Thickness: Roughly 5 mm (excluding through-hole header pins)

Direct Comparison: BPI-M2 Zero vs. Raspberry Pi Zero Series

The BPI-M2 Zero was engineered as a drop-in physical replacement for the Raspberry Pi Zero and Raspberry Pi Zero W.

  • Footprint and Mounting Holes: The outer dimensions, rounded corner radii, and four M2.5 mounting hole positions match the Raspberry Pi Zero specification exactly, allowing the BPI-M2 Zero to fit into most standard Raspberry Pi Zero enclosures.
  • 40-Pin Header: It features a standard 40-pin GPIO header layout with the same 2.54 mm pitch and physical location along the edge of the board.
  • Port Placement: The board features two micro-USB ports (one for OTG/data, one for DC power) and one mini-HDMI port placed along the long bottom edge, mirroring the configuration of the Raspberry Pi Zero.

Physical Differences from the Raspberry Pi Zero

Despite the identical outer dimensions, key layout variations exist:

  • Antenna Connector: Unlike the PCB trace antenna on the Raspberry Pi Zero W, the BPI-M2 Zero features an onboard u.FL/IPEX RF connector, allowing for an external antenna.
  • Physical Buttons: The BPI-M2 Zero includes soldered surface-mount Power and Reset buttons on the board edges, which are absent on standard Raspberry Pi Zero boards.
  • Component Clearance: Variations in component height and placement (such as the Allwinner H2+/H3 SoC and RAM chip) mean tight-fitting aluminum heatsink cases designed exclusively for Raspberry Pi chips may not make direct thermal contact without modification.

Comparison with Other Ultra-Compact Boards

Orange Pi Zero Series

While also labeled "Zero," the Orange Pi Zero line (including Zero LTS, Zero 2, and Zero 3) uses an entirely different, squarer footprint (approximately 48 mm × 46 mm to 53 mm × 60 mm).

  • Thickness: Orange Pi Zero boards are significantly thicker due to integrated full-sized USB-A ports and standard RJ45 Ethernet jacks.
  • Portability: The BPI-M2 Zero has a much slimmer, low-profile design ideal for embedded, flat enclosures where vertical clearance is limited.

Radxa Zero

The Radxa Zero shares the identical 65 mm × 30 mm form factor with the BPI-M2 Zero, but updates the connectivity standard.

  • USB Interfaces: Radxa Zero replaces micro-USB with modern USB Type-C ports.
  • Display Output: Radxa utilizes micro-HDMI rather than the mini-HDMI found on the BPI-M2 Zero.

Form Factor Summary

The Banana Pi BPI-M2 Zero strictly adheres to the 65 mm × 30 mm micro-SBC form factor standard. It is identical in size and port alignment to the Raspberry Pi Zero platform, while offering physical power/reset switches and an external antenna port, maintaining an ultra-thin profile compared to chunkier alternatives in the micro-SBC market.
