How SBCs Interface with Underwater Acoustic Sensors

Single board computers (SBCs) serve as compact, cost-effective processing hubs that enable real-time data acquisition and edge computing for underwater acoustic sensors in marine science research. By bridging the gap between analog hydrophones and digital data networks, SBCs allow researchers to record, process, and transmit underwater soundscapes—ranging from marine mammal vocalizations to anthropogenic noise—without requiring bulky, power-intensive computing systems.

Signal Conditioning and Digitization

Underwater acoustic sensors, primarily hydrophones, detect acoustic pressure waves and convert them into raw analog voltage signals. Because these signals are often weak and susceptible to high-frequency ocean noise, they must pass through a preamplifier and an analog bandpass filter before reaching the SBC.

Since most standard SBCs lack high-resolution analog inputs, the conditioned signal is digitized using high-speed Analog-to-Digital Converters (ADCs) or dedicated audio interface hardware. These digitizers communicate with the SBC through standard digital buses:

  • I2S (Inter-IC Sound): Preferred for continuous, low-latency audio streaming directly into the processor.
  • SPI (Serial Peripheral Interface): Used for precision, multi-channel ADCs sampling at high frequencies.
  • USB/Ethernet: Used when connecting commercial off-the-shelf acoustic recorders or digital hydrophones that have integrated digitizers.

Edge Processing and Data Handling

Once digitized, the acoustic data is processed locally by the SBC's central processor or onboard graphics/neural processing units. Using lightweight languages such as C++ or Python alongside signal-processing libraries, SBCs perform immediate tasks at the edge:

  • Fast Fourier Transforms (FFT): Converting time-domain sound signals into frequency spectra for noise characterization.
  • Feature Detection: Running lightweight machine learning models to automatically detect and classify biological events, such as dolphin clicks or whale songs.
  • Data Compression: Encoding raw acoustic streams into formats like FLAC or custom compressed binaries to conserve onboard storage.

Timing and Synchronization

Accurate temporal alignment is critical for acoustic localization and beamforming applications. Because SBCs typically do not have internet access underwater for Network Time Protocol (NTP) synchronization, they interface with high-precision Real-Time Clock (RTC) modules with temperature-compensated crystal oscillators (TCXO). When deployed in surface buoys or autonomous vehicles, SBCs sync their internal clocks to GPS time pulses before submerging.

Power and Housing Integration

Marine deployments demand strict energy budgets. SBCs are programmed to manage power by implementing duty-cycle schedules, putting both the computer and the sensor payload into deep-sleep modes between recording intervals. The entire assembly—including the SBC, power regulators, battery packs, and signal conditioning boards—is sealed inside waterproof pressure housings, with external hydrophones connected via underwater-rated bulkheads to withstand ocean pressure.
