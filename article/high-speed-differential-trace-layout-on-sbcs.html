High-Speed Differential Trace Layout on SBCs

High-speed differential trace routing on a Single Board Computer (SBC) is vital for protocols such as PCIe, USB, Ethernet, HDMI, and MIPI to function reliably without data corruption or excessive electromagnetic interference (EMI). Designing these traces on dense SBC layouts requires precise control over differential impedance, intra-pair skew, continuous reference planes, layer transitions, and spacing to minimize crosstalk and preserve signal integrity.

Controlled Differential Impedance

Maintain consistent trace width and intra-pair spacing throughout the entire run to achieve the target differential impedance—typically 90 ohms for USB or 100 ohms for PCIe, Ethernet, and LVDS. Any sudden variation in dielectric thickness, trace width, or separation creates impedance discontinuities, which cause signal reflections and degrade edge rates.

Intra-Pair Length Matching and Skew Minimization

The positive and negative traces of a differential pair must be matched in length to prevent phase skew, which converts differential signals into common-mode noise and increases EMI. Apply length-tuning serpentines as close as possible to the point where the length mismatch occurs, such as near angled bends or mismatched IC package breakout pins. Ensure the amplitude and spacing of serpentines do not overly decouple the pair.

Continuous Reference Planes

Differential signals rely on the nearest reference plane for their high-frequency return currents. Route high-speed differential pairs over a continuous, uninterrupted ground plane. Never route pairs over splits, voids, or power plane boundaries. Crossing a plane split forces the return current to take a long, inductive detour, causing impedance spikes and severe EMI.

Symmetrical Via Transitions

When routing high-speed pairs between layers, place the signal vias symmetrically and in close proximity. Add adjacent ground stitching vias right next to the signal vias to provide a continuous, low-inductance return path for the high-frequency currents. On multi-layer SBCs with high data rates (such as PCIe Gen 4/5), back-drilling or blind/buried vias should be utilized to eliminate unneeded via stubs that act as resonant antennas.

Isolation and Crosstalk Mitigation

Keep differential pairs isolated from adjacent high-speed pairs, single-ended traces, switching power supplies, and clock lines. Follow the "3W rule" (spacing between adjacent traces should be at least three times the trace width) or increase spacing to 5W near sensitive analog lines or noisy components to suppress near-end (NEXT) and far-end (FEXT) crosstalk.

Route Symmetry and Cornering

Route the pair together as a closely coupled unit, avoiding sharp 90-degree corners that introduce parasitic capacitance and radiation. Use 45-degree chamfers or curved circular arcs when turning. Ensure any unavoidable obstacle (such as a via or mounting hole) is bypassed symmetrically by both traces simultaneously rather than splitting the traces around opposite sides.

Symmetrical Component Placement

Passive components along the path, such as AC coupling capacitors or ESD protection diodes, must be placed symmetrically with identical footprints and pad sizes for both traces. Remove reference ground plane copper directly underneath surface-mount pads (cutouts) if necessary to compensate for excess parasitic pad capacitance and maintain the nominal differential impedance.
