Single Board Computers in Automated Greenhouses

Modern automated greenhouse facilities increasingly rely on single board computers (SBCs) to manage complex environmental variables and optimize crop yield. This article examines the central role SBCs play in greenhouse control systems, covering their application in real-time sensor monitoring, automated actuation, local edge processing, networking, and cost-effective system scaling.

Real-Time Environmental Monitoring

Single board computers act as the central data acquisition hubs within greenhouse zones. Equipped with general-purpose input/output (GPIO) pins and standard communication buses (such as I2C, SPI, and UART), SBCs interface directly with an array of digital and analog sensors. They continuously read critical environmental metrics, including:

  • Ambient air temperature and relative humidity
  • Soil moisture and electrical conductivity (EC)
  • Carbon dioxide (\(CO_2\)) concentration levels
  • Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR) and ambient light levels
  • pH levels in hydroponic nutrient reservoirs

Closed-Loop Actuation and Control

Beyond gathering data, SBCs execute programmatic logic to maintain setpoints without human intervention. By interfacing with relay modules, motor drivers, and solid-state switches, the SBC can trigger mechanical systems based on sensor thresholds:

  • Climate Regulation: Activating exhaust fans, evaporative cooling pads, louvers, and supplemental heating units to stabilize temperature.
  • Automated Irrigation: Opening solenoid valves to deliver water or nutrient solutions when soil moisture drops below targeted levels.
  • Dynamic Lighting: Deploying motorized shade curtains during peak sunlight or engaging supplemental LED grow lights during low-light conditions.
  • Air Circulation: Cycling horizontal airflow fans to prevent microclimates and fungal development.

Edge Computing and Local Intelligence

SBCs provide sufficient onboard processing power to execute control algorithms locally rather than relying exclusively on cloud infrastructure. This edge-computing capability ensures that:

  • Reliability Remains High: Critical routines (such as frost protection or emergency venting) continue functioning even if external internet connectivity fails.
  • Advanced Logic Can Run: SBCs can run Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) controllers, predictive models, or lightweight machine learning scripts to forecast heating demands or detect irrigation leaks.
  • Data Preprocessing Occurs Locally: High-frequency raw sensor data is filtered, averaged, and packaged before being stored or transmitted, reducing network bandwidth requirements.

Connectivity and Centralized Management

SBCs bridge field-level hardware with broader farm management software. Using integrated Wi-Fi, Ethernet, or cellular modules, they transmit operational telemetry to central servers or cloud platforms using lightweight protocols like MQTT, HTTP/REST, or CoAP. This connectivity allows operators to:

  • Monitor live conditions via web dashboards or mobile applications.
  • Receive automated alerts via SMS or email when parameters breach safe operating ranges.
  • Update environmental recipes and setpoints remotely across multiple greenhouse bays.

Scalability and Cost Efficiency

Compared to traditional, proprietary Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs), single board computers offer a modular and low-cost alternative. Their open-source software ecosystems and widespread hardware availability enable commercial growers and agricultural engineers to design custom, scalable automation architectures tailored to specific crop requirements without prohibitive licensing or deployment costs.
