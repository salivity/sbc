BeagleBone Black Wireless PCB Antenna Integration

The BeagleBone Black Wireless replaces the traditional wired Ethernet interface of the standard BeagleBone with 802.11b/g/n 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi and Bluetooth 4.1. This article explores how the board's printed circuit board (PCB) integrates its wireless antenna system, detailing the use of an onboard ceramic chip antenna, ground plane keep-out zones, controlled impedance routing, and the selectable U.FL connector for external antenna options.

Texas Instruments WiLink 8 Module Integration

At the core of the BeagleBone Black Wireless’s RF system is the Texas Instruments WL1835MOD module. Rather than routing raw RF silicon directly on the main board, the WL1835MOD incorporates the transceiver, power amplifier, low-noise amplifier, power management, and RF shielding within a single surface-mount package. The module outputs an unbalanced 50-ohm RF signal that is routed across the host PCB to the antenna section.

Onboard Ceramic Chip Antenna Placement

The primary integrated antenna is a miniature surface-mount ceramic chip antenna tuned for the 2.4 GHz ISM band.

  • Board Edge Positioning: The chip antenna is placed along the outer corner of the PCB, away from high-speed digital buses, the AM3358 SoC, and the DDR3 memory to minimize electromagnetic interference (EMI).
  • Ground Plane Keep-Out: Ceramic chip antennas require a specific clearance area to radiate efficiently. The layout enforces a strict copper keep-out zone directly beneath and surrounding the antenna on all internal and external PCB layers. This prevents the ground planes from shorting the radiated near-field electromagnetic energy.

RF Trace Routing and Impedance Matching

The connection between the WL1835MOD RF pin and the antenna consists of a controlled-impedance microstrip line:

  1. 50-Ohm Characteristic Impedance: The RF trace width and clearance are precisely calculated based on the PCB's dielectric constant (FR-4) and layer stackup height to maintain a 50-ohm impedance, minimizing signal reflection and insertion loss.
  2. Matching Network: A passive LC matching network (composed of surface-mount capacitors and inductors) is placed along the trace before the antenna. This circuit fine-tunes the resonant frequency to 2.4 GHz and accounts for parasitics introduced by the PCB substrate and surrounding components.

External Antenna U.FL Connector Option

To accommodate enclosures or extended-range applications, the PCB layout includes footprints for both the onboard ceramic antenna and an alternative U.FL (micro-coaxial) connector.

  • Solder Jumper/0-Ohm Resistor Switching: The board uses a 0-ohm surface-mount resistor (or capacitor bridge) configured as an RF switch. In the standard factory configuration, the bridge routes the 50-ohm line to the ceramic chip antenna.
  • Reconfiguration: Users can desolder and rotate the 0-ohm jumper to disconnect the internal chip antenna and redirect the RF path to the adjacent U.FL connector, allowing an external antenna to be used without causing impedance mismatches or stub reflections.

Layer Isolation and Noise Management

Because single-board computers generate significant switching noise from power management ICs (PMIC) and digital clock signals, the wireless section relies on solid ground reference planes directly beneath the RF routing layer up to the antenna keep-out boundary. Stitching vias are placed along the perimeter of the RF trace to suppress stray RF radiation and isolate the analog wireless signals from adjacent digital signals.
