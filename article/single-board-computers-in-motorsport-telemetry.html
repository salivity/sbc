Single Board Computers in Motorsport Telemetry

Single-board computers (SBCs) have become pivotal components in modern motorsport, serving as compact, cost-effective edge-computing hubs for localized vehicle telemetry tracking. By bridging the gap between raw automotive sensor networks and actionable driver performance metrics, these devices enable teams to capture, process, and analyze complex vehicle dynamics directly on the vehicle in real time, bypassing the latency and bandwidth limitations of remote cloud infrastructure.

Real-Time Data Ingestion and Sensor Fusion

Competitive race cars generate vast amounts of data across multiple communication protocols. SBCs such as the Raspberry Pi, BeagleBone, and NVIDIA Jetson integrate with Controller Area Network (CAN bus) interfaces, OBD-II systems, and analog-to-digital converters (ADCs).

By connecting directly to the vehicle's engine control unit (ECU) and secondary sensor networks, an SBC aggregates diverse data streams simultaneously, including:

  • Engine metrics: RPM, throttle position, boost pressure, and fluid temperatures.
  • Chassis dynamics: Wheel speed, brake pressure, suspension travel, and steering angle.
  • Spatial and inertial tracking: High-frequency GPS data combined with 6-axis or 9-axis Inertial Measurement Units (IMU) to measure pitch, roll, yaw, and G-forces.

Through sensor fusion algorithms, the SBC aligns timestamps across all these inputs to create a cohesive snapshot of vehicle behavior through every corner and straightaway.

Edge Processing and Dynamic Calculations

Instead of simply acting as dumb loggers, SBCs function as edge processors. They execute computationally intensive scripts locally while the vehicle is on the track.

Localized processing allows the system to calculate:

  • Tire Slip Angles and Traction Limits: Comparing wheel speed to GPS-derived ground speed to alert drivers or log understeer and oversteer conditions.
  • Predictive Lap Times: Real-time calculation of sector deltas based on reference laps.
  • Thermal Modeling: Calculating dynamic tire and brake wear based on temperature profiles and sustained load.

Performing these computations onboard ensures zero latency for in-cabin driver displays, shift lights, and immediate acoustic warnings.

Fail-Safe Data Logging

Wireless connections on racing circuits are frequently disrupted by track topography, physical barriers, and electromagnetic interference. SBCs provide localized solid-state storage (via high-endurance eMMC, NVMe SSDs, or SD cards) to record telemetry at high polling rates (often 50Hz to 1000Hz). This local-first architecture guarantees complete data integrity, ensuring no telemetry points are lost if live telemetry transmission drops out.

Localized Telemetry Transmission

For pit-crew monitoring, SBCs manage low-latency, localized transmission networks. Depending on the track configuration and series regulations, the SBC can route parsed telemetry through:

  • Long-Range Radio (LoRa) or ISM Band Transceivers: Transmitting critical telemetry packets over several kilometers without relying on public cellular infrastructure.
  • Point-to-Point Wi-Fi: Offloading gigabytes of detailed diagnostic logs automatically when the car enters pit lane.
  • Private LTE/5G Gateways: Streaming real-time dashboards to the engineering team on the pit wall.

Customizability and Cost-to-Performance Ratio

Traditional motorsport data acquisition systems often rely on proprietary hardware and locked software ecosystems that carry significant price premiums. SBCs provide an open-architecture alternative running Linux-based environments. This allows race engineers to develop custom dashboards, tailor logging algorithms in Python or C++, and deploy machine learning models at the edge without vendor lock-in. Their small form factor, minimal weight, and low power consumption make them ideal for the strict packaging constraints of competitive race vehicles.
