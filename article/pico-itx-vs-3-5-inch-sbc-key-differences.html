Pico-ITX vs 3.5-Inch SBC: Key Differences

When designing embedded systems, selecting the correct form factor is critical to balancing physical constraints with processing capabilities. Both Pico-ITX and 3.5-inch single board computers (SBCs) are widely used industrial computing standards, yet they serve distinct operational needs. The primary differences between them lie in their physical footprint, thermal performance, processor scalability, I/O connectivity, and power design. While Pico-ITX focuses on ultra-compact efficiency for extreme space limitations, the 3.5-inch standard provides a balanced blend of high compute performance, rich native connectivity, and flexible expansion.

Dimensions and Footprint

The most immediate distinction is size:

  • Pico-ITX: Measures 100 mm × 72 mm (3.9 in × 2.8 in). Originally defined by VIA Technologies, it is roughly half the size of a Nano-ITX board and represents one of the smallest standard x86 and ARM SBC formats available.
  • 3.5-inch SBC: Measures 146 mm × 102 mm (5.75 in × 4.0 in). Despite its name, the dimensions are derived from the footprint of a standard 3.5-inch magnetic disk drive rather than the board's diagonal length.

The smaller footprint of Pico-ITX allows it to fit into extremely tight enclosures, handheld devices, and compact drones. In contrast, the 3.5-inch board requires more physical volume but offers significantly more surface area for integrated components.

Processing Power and Thermal Management

The physical area of the board directly dictates the thermal design power (TDP) it can dissipate and, consequently, the class of processors it can host.

  • Pico-ITX: Generally equipped with ultra-low-power system-on-chips (SoCs), such as Intel Atom, Celeron, or low-power ARM architectures, typically operating within a 5W to 15W TDP range. Because surface area for heatsinks is limited, these boards are built for low-heat, fanless operation where basic compute efficiency is prioritized over raw performance.
  • 3.5-inch SBC: Capable of housing high-performance industrial processors, including Intel Core i3/i5/i7/i9, Intel Xeon, and AMD Ryzen Embedded processors, often handling 15W to 45W+ TDP. The larger surface area accommodates substantial heatsinks, heat spreaders, or active cooling fans, making it capable of running heavy workloads such as real-time edge AI inference and multi-stream video processing.

I/O Connectivity and Expansion

PCB real estate determines how many physical connectors can fit directly onto the board edges versus requiring ribbon cables or external daughterboards.

  • Pico-ITX: Due to edge-space constraints, Pico-ITX boards typically provide only essential external connectors (e.g., one RJ45 Ethernet port, one or two USB ports, and a single HDMI/Micro-HDMI output). Additional interfaces such as COM ports, audio, and GPIOs are routed through internal pin headers. Expansion is limited, often offering only a single M.2 slot (such as M.2 Key-E for Wi-Fi) or proprietary mezzanine expansion boards.
  • 3.5-inch SBC: Offers extensive native I/O directly on the rear I/O panel, commonly featuring dual or quad Gigabit/2.5GbE Ethernet ports, multiple USB 3.2 ports, multiple display outputs (DisplayPort, HDMI, LVDS/eDP), and dedicated RS-232/422/485 serial ports. Expansion is robust, frequently including multiple standard M.2 slots (Key-M for NVMe storage, Key-B for 4G/5G, Key-E for wireless), full-size Mini PCIe slots, and SIM card sockets.

Power Input and Industrial Ruggedization

  • Pico-ITX: Frequently designed to operate on a fixed single-voltage direct current (DC) input, typically +12V DC or +5V DC. While industrial-grade versions exist with wide-temperature support, the compact component layout leaves limited room for extensive power protection circuitry.
  • 3.5-inch SBC: Purpose-built for rugged industrial environments, these boards regularly feature wide-range DC power inputs (such as 9V to 36V DC) alongside integrated over-voltage, over-current, and reverse-polarity protection to handle unstable power sources in factory and automotive settings.

Summary of Best-Fit Applications

  • Choose Pico-ITX when: Space is the primary design constraint, power consumption must be minimized, and compute requirements are focused on lightweight tasks like smart sensors, basic IoT gateways, portable medical instruments, and compact robotics.
  • Choose 3.5-inch SBC when: The application demands desktop-class computing performance, multiple high-speed camera or network inputs, multi-display support, wide-temperature industrial reliability, and diverse expansion options for machine vision, industrial automation, edge AI, and digital signage.
