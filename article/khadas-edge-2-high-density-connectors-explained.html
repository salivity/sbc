Khadas Edge2 High-Density Connectors Explained

The Khadas Edge2 single board computer utilizes high-density expansion connectors to route high-speed signals, display interfaces, camera inputs, and general-purpose I/O while preserving an ultra-slim form factor. By replacing bulky traditional pin headers with compact, surface-mount board-to-board connectors, the Edge2 enables developers to access the full capabilities of the Rockchip RK3588S system-on-chip (SoC) for embedded systems, robotics, and edge AI applications without adding vertical bulk.

Form Factor and Hardware Design

Traditional single board computers often rely on 2.54mm pitch 40-pin headers, which significantly increase the overall thickness of the board. The Khadas Edge2 eliminates this bulk by employing dual high-density, low-profile connectors on the underside of the PCB. This engineering choice allows the device to maintain a thickness of just 5.7 millimeters, making it suitable for integration into tightly constrained enclosures, handheld equipment, and compact drone or robotic chassis.

High-Bandwidth Display and Camera Interfaces

The high-density connectors route the advanced multimedia capabilities of the RK3588S SoC directly to external modules:

  • MIPI-CSI (Camera Serial Interface): The connectors provide multi-lane MIPI-CSI interfaces, allowing developers to connect high-resolution camera sensors simultaneously. This enables computer vision applications, stereo depth sensing, and multi-angle surveillance.
  • MIPI-DSI (Display Serial Interface): High-speed display lanes run through these connectors, supporting embedded LCD panels and touchscreen modules without requiring standard, bulky HDMI cables.

Low-Speed Bus and Peripheral Integration

Beyond high-speed video and imaging channels, the high-density connectors expose a wide array of standard embedded protocols. These include:

  • UART, I2C, and SPI: Essential for communicating with microcontrollers, motor drivers, digital sensors, and external EEPROMs.
  • PWM and ADC: Utilized for reading analog inputs and generating pulse-width modulation signals to drive servos, fans, or lighting.
  • GPIO (General Purpose Input/Output): Configurable digital pins used for custom hardware triggering, buttons, and status indicators.

Modular Carrier Board Compatibility

The connectors serve as the primary docking interface for carrier boards and breakout accessories. When mated with accessories such as the Edge2 I/O Board, the high-density lines are converted into standard physical interfaces, including traditional 40-pin headers, Ethernet ports, TF card slots, and audio jacks. This modularity allows the Khadas Edge2 to function as a plug-and-play development board on the workbench or as a surface-mounted compute module in production hardware.
