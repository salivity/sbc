Khadas VIM4 Active Cooling Under Peak Loads

The Khadas VIM4 single board computer manages demanding computational tasks by combining a dedicated hardware cooling kit with intelligent, kernel-level thermal management. This article examines how the VIM4 detects temperature spikes across its octa-core processor, dynamically regulates its pulse-width modulation (PWM) fan, and utilizes forced-air convection to prevent thermal throttling and maintain maximum performance under sustained peak workloads.

Thermal Monitoring and Sensor Integration

At the core of the Khadas VIM4's cooling strategy is the Amlogic A311D2 SoC, which integrates high-precision thermal sensors embedded directly near the performance CPU cores (quad Cortex-A73) and the Mali-G52 MP8 GPU. The operating system kernel constantly queries these internal sensors through thermal zones. When intensive multi-threaded tasks, 4K/8K video processing, or AI workloads cause rapid heat generation, the thermal subsystem detects the rising junction temperature in real time.

Intelligent PWM Fan Regulation

Rather than running at a fixed, noisy speed, the VIM4 utilizes a 3705 active cooling fan driven by Pulse Width Modulation (PWM). The cooling algorithm operates on predefined temperature trip points:

  • Idle / Low Load (< 50°C): The fan remains off or spins at minimal RPM to ensure near-silent operation while natural conduction through the aluminum heatsink handles baseline heat.
  • Moderate Load (50°C – 65°C): As processing demands rise, the system engages the fan at low to mid-range duty cycles, establishing smooth airflow without sudden acoustic spikes.
  • Peak Load (> 65°C): When the SoC approaches critical operating temperatures under sustained 100% core utilization, the firmware pushes the PWM duty cycle to maximum speed (up to roughly 4,000–5,000 RPM), maximizing cubic feet per minute (CFM) across the cooling fins.

Conductive and Convective Heat Dissipation

The hardware architecture relies on a two-stage heat dissipation process. First, high-conductivity thermal pads transfer heat from the SoC, RAM, and power management IC directly into a custom-machined aluminum heatsink. Second, the low-profile 3705 centrifugal blower draws cool air from the sides and forces it horizontally across the dense fin array. This active convection rapidly carries heat away from the board surface, protecting nearby surface-mount components and power delivery circuits from heat soak.

Prevention of Thermal Throttling

Without active cooling, high-performance ARM SoCs quickly reach their thermal ceiling (typically around 80°C to 85°C), forcing the dynamic voltage and frequency scaling (DVFS) governor to throttle clock speeds to prevent hardware damage.

Under maximum sustained synthetic stress tests, the VIM4's active cooling system stabilizes junction temperatures well below the critical throttling threshold. By holding the operating temperature in a safe 60°C to 72°C window, the VIM4 allows all CPU and GPU cores to continuously operate at their maximum rated clock frequencies without experiencing dropped frames, processing latency, or performance degradation.
