Single Board Computers in Portable Ultrasound Devices

Single Board Computers (SBCs) serve as the computational backbone of modern portable ultrasound systems, integrating processors, memory, and input/output interfaces onto a single circuit board. This article explores how SBCs enable point-of-care ultrasound (POCUS) devices by handling real-time digital signal processing, driving compact user interfaces, optimizing power consumption, and facilitating secure medical data transmission in decentralized healthcare environments.

Real-Time Signal and Image Processing

In portable ultrasound systems, raw acoustic data captured by piezoelectric transducers must be instantly converted into readable diagnostic images. SBCs equipped with integrated heterogeneous processors—combining multi-core CPUs, GPUs, and Digital Signal Processors (DSPs)—perform high-speed digital beamforming, filtering, and scan conversion. This computational power ensures high frame rates and precise spatial resolution without the need for bulky, separate processing units.

Form Factor and Thermal Efficiency

Point-of-care ultrasound devices require compact, lightweight enclosures to maximize mobility for emergency response, bedside diagnostics, and remote clinics. Modern SBCs offer ultra-small form factors (such as COM Express, SMARC, or proprietary embedded designs) that fit within handheld or laptop-style chassis. Furthermore, their low-power architecture reduces thermal output, enabling fanless designs that prevent overheating, maintain sterile operating conditions, and extend battery runtimes.

Control, User Interface, and Operating Systems

SBCs manage the overall system architecture, running embedded operating systems such as specialized Linux distributions or Windows Embedded. They control peripheral interfaces, allowing seamless integration with touchscreens, physical dials, battery management systems, and probe connectors. The SBC processes user inputs in real time, adjusting gain, depth, frequency, and Doppler modes instantaneously while rendering intuitive graphical user interfaces for clinicians.

Connectivity and Telemedicine Capabilities

Modern diagnostic workflows require rapid sharing and storage of patient scans. SBCs feature built-in wireless modules (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and 5G cellular connectivity) and standard interfaces (USB-C, Ethernet) that enable ultrasound devices to interface directly with Picture Archiving and Communication Systems (PACS). They also facilitate secure data encryption for HIPAA compliance and power real-time telemedicine platforms, allowing remote specialists to view live ultrasound feeds.

Cost Reduction and Modularity

Using standardized, medical-grade SBCs simplifies the hardware design and regulatory certification process for medical device manufacturers. Instead of designing proprietary multi-board systems from scratch, manufacturers can leverage modular SBCs to accelerate development cycles, lower manufacturing costs, and easily upgrade processing components as newer, more efficient silicon architectures become available.
