Odroid-C4 Efficiency Per Watt vs Modern SBCs

The Odroid-C4, powered by the Amlogic S905X3 processor featuring four ARM Cortex-A55 cores, remains a strong benchmark for energy-efficient computing. This article analyzes how the Odroid-C4 compares to modern single-board computers (SBCs)—such as the Raspberry Pi 5, Orange Pi 5, and newer entry-level boards—in terms of power consumption, idle efficiency, and raw compute efficiency per watt.

Baseline Power Profile of the Odroid-C4

The Odroid-C4 utilizes an energy-efficient 12nm manufacturing process. Its hardware architecture yields distinct power metrics:

  • Idle Power Consumption: Approximately 1.5W to 2.0W.
  • Full Load Power Consumption: Approximately 3.5W to 4.5W.
  • Thermal Profile: Passive cooling is sufficient for sustained operation under most workloads without thermal throttling.

Because the Cortex-A55 microarchitecture focuses on power optimization rather than high-performance execution, the Odroid-C4 maintains a minimal electrical footprint even under 100% CPU utilization.

Comparison with Modern High-Performance SBCs

Raspberry Pi 5 (Broadcom BCM2712)

The Raspberry Pi 5 uses quad Cortex-A76 cores manufactured on a 16nm process.

  • Power Draw: Idles around 3.0W to 5.0W and reaches 10W to 12W under heavy synthetic loads.
  • Efficiency per Watt: The Pi 5 delivers roughly two to three times the raw compute power of the Odroid-C4. For bursty or compute-heavy tasks (such as compiling code or running complex local scripts), the Pi 5 finishes significantly faster, achieving a better "energy per task" metric. However, for continuous, lightweight tasks, its higher baseline wattage makes it less energy-efficient over time compared to the C4.

Orange Pi 5 / RK3588S-Based Boards

Boards equipped with the Rockchip RK3588S use an 8nm big.LITTLE configuration (four Cortex-A76 and four Cortex-A55 cores).

  • Power Draw: Idles at roughly 2.5W to 3.5W and scales up to 8W–12W under maximum load.
  • Efficiency per Watt: The 8nm node and architectural advancements make the RK3588S substantially more efficient per watt than the Odroid-C4 under heavy multi-threaded workloads. While the peak power is higher, the compute throughput per watt gained from modern out-of-order execution cores easily outclasses the older S905X3.

Comparison with Direct Modern Peers (Cortex-A55 Class)

Modern budget alternatives using processors like the Rockchip RK3566 (e.g., Radxa Zero 3W or Orange Pi 3B) represent direct modern equivalents to the Odroid-C4.

  • Power Draw: Idle power ranges between 1.0W and 1.8W, with peak loads between 3.0W and 4.0W.
  • Efficiency per Watt: These modern budget boards perform almost identically to the Odroid-C4 in pure CPU tasks since they share the Cortex-A55 architecture. However, modern alternatives often integrate slightly updated memory controllers (LPDDR4/LPDDR4X) and newer NPU/crypto engines, offering a slight edge in specialized operations without increasing energy consumption.

Workload Suitability

  • Always-On Micro-Services (Pi-hole, Home Assistant, Lightweight NAS): The Odroid-C4 holds parity with modern budget alternatives. Its ultra-low idle and low-load consumption (sub-3W) make it significantly more cost-effective and energy-efficient in 24/7 standby than high-power modern boards like the Raspberry Pi 5.
  • Data Processing and Media Transcoding: Modern boards outclass the Odroid-C4. Newer architectures complete heavy instructions far faster, returning to low-power idle states quicker and using less overall energy per completed job.
