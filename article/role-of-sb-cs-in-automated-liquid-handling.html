Role of SBCs in Automated Liquid Handling

Single board computers (SBCs) serve as the primary computational and control hubs in modern automated laboratory liquid-handling instruments. By combining processors, memory, input/output interfaces, and network capabilities onto a single compact circuit board, SBCs manage complex multi-axis motion, monitor sensor feedback, execute pipetting protocols, and bridge laboratory automation workflows with centralized data systems.

Motion Control and Precision Dispensing

Automated liquid handlers rely on precise spatial positioning to move pipetting heads, microplates, and disposable tips across multi-axis coordinate systems (X, Y, and Z axes). SBCs coordinate these movements by interfacing directly with motor controllers via communication buses such as CAN, SPI, I2C, or UART. The onboard processor calculates acceleration profiles, path trajectories, and channel synchronization to aspirate and dispense microliter and nanoliter volumes of fluids without splashing, droplet retention, or positioning errors.

Sensor Integration and Real-Time Monitoring

Liquid-handling reliability depends heavily on active process verification. SBCs process signals from an array of integrated sensors, including:

  • Liquid Level Detection (LLD): Capacitive, pressure-based, or optical sensors that detect the surface of a liquid before aspiration.
  • Pressure Sensors: Real-time transducers that identify clogs, empty wells, or air bubbles during pipetting cycles.
  • Environmental Controls: Temperature, humidity, and airflow sensors for sensitive biochemical assays.

By handling these inputs, the SBC can trigger immediate error-recovery routines or halt operations to prevent sample loss and cross-contamination.

Protocol Execution and Workflow Automation

Modern liquid handlers need to execute intricate protocols that vary between experiments. Running full operating systems (typically embedded Linux), SBCs can host script interpreters (such as Python) and protocol engines. This allows the instrument to parse standard automation commands, manage scheduling algorithms, and execute complex workflows without requiring a dedicated external desktop computer.

Connectivity and LIMS Integration

SBCs provide built-in networking options, such as Ethernet and Wi-Fi, allowing liquid-handling instruments to function as connected IoT devices in smart laboratories. They interface directly with Laboratory Information Management Systems (LIMS) to download task queues, report run statuses, log error data, and track sample chain-of-custody. Additionally, onboard USB and display interfaces enable direct connection to touchscreens and graphical user interfaces (GUIs) for standalone operation.

Footprint, Cost, and Modularity

Compared to traditional industrial PCs, single board computers significantly reduce the physical footprint, thermal output, and manufacturing cost of automated workstations. Their modular nature allows instrument designers to upgrade processing capabilities or replace control hardware with minimal redesign of the underlying fluidic and mechanical assemblies.
